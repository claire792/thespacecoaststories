## More than a funeral guest book

A funeral guest book records who attended. A custom memorial book preserves who the person was: the phrases they always used, the work they were proud of, the food they made, the friendships they kept, and the small rituals their family misses most.

The book can begin before a celebration of life, but it does not have to be rushed. Many families collect names and memories at the service, then build the fuller story afterward when there is time to listen carefully.

## Build the portrait together

No single relative holds every story. A sibling remembers childhood. A friend remembers the reckless years. A colleague knows the work that shaped them. A child knows the private rituals that made a house feel like home.

We gather those perspectives, along with photographs and the person's own writings, and shape them into one book that feels whole.

Relatives do not have to live near one another. Interviews can be recorded separately by phone or video, then woven into a single narrative. That makes an online memorial book project especially useful when a family is spread across the country.

In-person interviews are available across Florida's Space Coast, while remote memorial projects can bring together relatives nationwide.

## Helpful materials

Letters, journals, recipes, military papers, favorite sayings, photographs, voicemail recordings, and stories shared at a celebration of life can all become part of the book.

Depending on what exists, the finished book may include a timeline, family tree, scanned handwriting, favorite recipes, excerpts from letters, captions, short memories from friends, and QR links to audio or video.
