## A family tree is the beginning, not the finished story

Genealogy can tell you that two people married in 1948, moved to Florida, and raised four children. A family history book tells you what the wedding looked like, why they moved, which child always escaped through the porch screen, and what Sunday dinner smelled like.

The records establish the facts. Interviews, letters, photographs, recipes, and family memories make those facts feel like a life.

## What can go into the book

A family history book can be as focused or expansive as your material allows. Common pieces include:

- A family tree or simplified lineage chart
- Short biographies of key relatives
- Chapters organized by generation, branch, place, or theme
- Census records, military documents, immigration papers, and maps
- Photographs with names, dates, and useful captions
- Recipes, letters, diary pages, and handwritten notes
- A timeline connecting family events to local or national history
- Recorded memories from living relatives
- A source list for future researchers

The goal is not to cram every record onto every page. It is to give readers a clear path through the family and make them want to keep turning pages.

## How the service works

First, we take inventory. You show Claire what already exists and explain what your family most wants to preserve. Then we choose a structure, identify the relatives who should be interviewed, and create a practical list of missing material.

Claire conducts the interviews, reviews the documents, writes and edits the chapters, and designs the pages. Your family reviews the draft for facts, spellings, relationships, and tone before anything is finalized.

## Choose a structure that fits the family

Some books begin with the earliest documented ancestor and move forward. Others begin with the living parent or grandparent and move backward. A family with deep roots in one community may organize the book around a farm, neighborhood, church, business, or migration.

There is no single correct format. The best structure is the one a reader can understand without already knowing the family tree.
