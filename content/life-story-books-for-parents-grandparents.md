## A parent should not have to become a writer

Many life story journals fail for a simple reason: they quietly assign homework to the person receiving the gift. A blank page arrives. The parent intends to fill it out. Weeks pass. The book stays blank.

A custom life story book removes that burden. Your parent or grandparent only needs to talk. Claire guides the conversation with questions written for their actual life, then handles the work that turns spoken memories into a readable book.

## What families receive

Depending on the package, the finished project can include:

- Recorded interviews and edited transcripts
- A written narrative that still sounds like the storyteller
- Family photographs, documents, and captions
- A timeline or family tree
- Favorite sayings, recipes, letters, or advice
- Professional page design
- A digital edition and printed copies

See [all packages and prices](/services/), from a one-hour Story Session to a Full Legacy Book.

## Good times to begin

Families often commission a book for an eightieth or ninetieth birthday, retirement, anniversary, family reunion, Mother's Day, Father's Day, or Christmas. Others begin after a health change makes the unanswered questions feel more urgent.

The book does not need to be finished before the occasion. Many families give the project itself as the gift, then let the interviews become part of the celebration.

## Start smaller if a whole life feels too big

A parent who resists a “memoir” may happily talk for one hour about the first house, the family business, the old neighborhood, military service, or the story of how they met.

That is why the smallest professional option is a [$399 Story Session](/story-sessions/): one recorded hour on one theme, turned into a finished booklet. It can stand alone or become the first chapter of a larger book later.

For a slower project that happens by ordinary phone calls, [Audio Letters](/audio-letters/) sends one personalized question at a time and builds the book gradually.
