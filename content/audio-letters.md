## The way it works

Once a week, a question goes out. Your parent calls a private line and hears it in a real voice, not a robot's: *"Hi Arthur, it's Claire. Last week you mentioned the boat your father built. This week I want to hear about the first time he let you take it out alone."*

They talk for as long as they want. They hang up.

We transcribe it, edit it lightly so it still sounds exactly like them, and write next week's question based on what they just said. That last part is the whole difference. The questions are not a list. They are a conversation that happens to be spread across a few months.

## Choose how long

**Four weeks — $249.** Four questions, four answers, a smaller designed keepsake, and a $100 credit toward a longer Audio Letters term or legacy book. A good first step when a birthday is close.

**Twelve weeks — $799.** Twelve stories with real range, a softcover book of roughly 60 pages, and enough material that patterns start to show up. This is the one most families pick.

**Twenty-four weeks — $1,399.** Half a year of stories, a full hardcover, and something close to a proper memoir. By month four most storytellers are volunteering things nobody in the family had ever heard.

Longer terms cost less per week, because the longer we go the better the book gets, and we'd rather you keep going.

## Why the phone

The people with the best stories are frequently the people least interested in typing them into a website. A phone is the one piece of technology they have never once needed instructions for.

Save the number in their contacts as "Claire — Story Line" and the entire user interface becomes one tap.

## What families say it feels like

Like getting a letter back. You send a question into someone's life and a few days later their voice comes back carrying something you didn't know. Most families tell us the recordings end up mattering as much as the book.
