## Pick one thing worth keeping

Most families stall out because "write down Dad's life" is too big to start. So we don't start there. We pick one thread and pull it all the way through.

- **The war years**, or the service, or the part he never talks about
- **How they met** and what the first apartment looked like
- **The work** — the shop, the launch pad, the classroom, the boat
- **The house you grew up in**, room by room
- **The recipe**, and the woman who taught it to her
- **The year everything changed**

You choose the theme. We build the questions around it and send them ahead of time if the storyteller wants to see them, or keep them a surprise if that works better.

## How a session runs

**Before.** A short call with you, the family member arranging it, to learn who we're talking to and what you're hoping to hear. Fifteen minutes.

**The hour.** A recorded conversation in your home or facility on the Space Coast, or by phone or video anywhere in the United States. It is a conversation, not an interrogation. We follow the memories that light them up.

**After.** We transcribe, shape it into readable chapters that still sound like them, place your photographs, and design a 15-page booklet. You review it, we fix anything wrong, and then it's printed.

## Two sessions, if one isn't enough

One session is $399. Two sessions on two themes are $749 and come back as one longer booklet. Every session you buy earns $75 toward your next one, so a family can build this a piece at a time instead of writing one large check.

And if it turns into something bigger, nothing is wasted. The full price of your first session comes off a Mini or Full Legacy Book commissioned within a year.

## Why this instead of a subscription service

Automated services email a question every week and file whatever comes back. Nobody edits it. Nobody asks the follow-up. What you get at the end is a stack of raw answers.

A Story Session has a person on the other side who has done this before, knows when to push gently, and knows which forty minutes of the hour are the ones that matter.
